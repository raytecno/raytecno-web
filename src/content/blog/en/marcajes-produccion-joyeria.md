# Production tracking in jewelry: why knowing what happens in the workshop in real time changes everything

In most jewelry workshops, the hardest question to answer isn't *how much a piece costs*, but something seemingly simpler: **where is each order right now and who has it in their hands?**

Anyone who has run a workshop knows it. Gold enters casting, moves to filing, then to barrels, polishing, setting, plating, finishing… and at each jump there's a moment when the piece "disappears" from the radar. Not physically —it's on a tray, in an envelope, on someone's bench— but in terms of information: nobody knows exactly when it entered that phase, how long it's been waiting, how much gold it weighed at the start and how much it weighs now. That blind spot is where delivery deadlines are lost, where losses slip away, and where it becomes impossible to know the real cost of manufacturing.

**Shop-floor data capture** —what at RayGold we call **production tracking**— exists to eliminate that blind spot. And although it sounds like a technical detail, it's probably the change with the most impact on the day-to-day of a jewelry workshop.

## The problem: a workshop that runs "from memory"

Let's think about how production is controlled in a workshop that hasn't yet taken this step.

The most common approach is the **paper sheet**: the manufacturing envelope carries a sheet where, in theory, each operator notes what they do. In practice, those sheets get filled in late, in hurried handwriting, sometimes at the end of the day reconstructing from memory, and often not filled in at all. The result is an incomplete record that reaches the office days late and rarely adds up.

The "modern" alternative is usually a **spreadsheet** where someone dumps those sheets. It improves legibility, but carries the same underlying problem: the data is entered *afterward*, one person centralizes it, and by the time it's in the computer it's no longer useful for deciding anything —only for confirming what already happened.

In both cases, the workshop runs from memory. And that has very concrete consequences for a jewelry business:

- **Real times are unknown.** You know what each phase *should* take, but not what it really takes. That's why delivery deadlines are promised by guesswork and missed without knowing why.
- **Gold losses are invisible until it's too late.** If weight is only checked at the start and end of the complete process, an abnormal loss in an intermediate phase is detected weeks later —when it can no longer be attributed to anything specific.
- **The bottleneck is a suspicion, not data.** Everyone in the workshop "senses" which phase is slower, but nobody can prove it with numbers, so it's impossible to justify where to invest.
- **The real cost of each piece is an estimate.** Without real hours per phase, the manufacturing cost is calculated with theoretical times that rarely match reality. And in jewelry, where margin and the gold price condition everything, that gap translates directly into money.

## The solution: let the data be born at the workstation, at the exact moment

Production tracking reverses the order of things. Instead of reconstructing what happened, **the data is captured at the very instant it occurs, at the workstation itself and by the operator themselves.**

The idea is deliberately simple, because it has to work in a real workshop, with hands busy and without interrupting the work. When the operator starts working on a batch, they make an **entry tracking**; when they finish it and pass it to the next phase, they make an **exit tracking**. That's all. But that minimal gesture, repeated along the entire manufacturing route, generates the information that didn't exist before.

And it does so by adapting to how each workshop works, not the other way around. At RayGold, the operator can track in several ways depending on the workstation:

- **By reading the code on the manufacturing envelope** with a barcode or QR reader. The envelope already identifies the plan, line and item, so a simple scan is enough to record the movement without typing anything.
- **From a tablet or touch screen** installed at the workstation, designed to be used with a couple of taps.
- **From the operator's own phone**, especially useful for external operators or sections where fixing a screen makes no sense.

The operator chooses what's most convenient for their section, and everything feeds the same system.

## What each tracking records (and why it matters)

A tracking isn't just "so-and-so touched this piece." It captures three things that, together, are the raw material of all production control:

**Real times per phase.** By recording entry and exit, the system knows exactly how long each batch has been *in process* in a phase and, just as revealing, how long it's been *waiting* between one phase and the next. In most workshops, the waiting time between phases far exceeds the effective working time —and until it's measured, nobody is aware of it.

**Weight and losses.** In jewelry, control can't be limited to time: you have to follow the gold. Tracking allows you to record the **entry and exit weight** of each phase and, with it, the **loss** produced at each step. This completely changes precious metal management: instead of discovering at the end that "a few grams are missing," you see in which specific phase and with which operator each loss occurred, and you can compare it with the tolerable loss of that process.

**Who, when and where.** Each tracking is associated with the operator —internal or external— who performed it. Not to watch over anyone, but to be able to analyze performance by section, detect where rework concentrates, and recognize which parts of the process work well.

## The quality leap: from data to the real-time Factory Map

This is where data capture stops being an administrative record and becomes a management tool.

Each tracking an operator makes on the shop floor **is reflected instantly on the Factory Map**: the visual view of the workshop where you see, phase by phase, what's in process, what's waiting and what has already moved on. When an operator marks the exit from polishing, the piece "moves" on the map at that very moment. The production manager doesn't have to call anyone or go down to the workshop to know how an order is doing: they see it on screen, live.

That same information, accumulated, is what later feeds the **production analysis**: the real cycle times, the bottleneck proven with numbers, the deviations between planned and executed, the gold balance of each plan. None of that would be possible without the source data. **Tracking is the foundation on which all manufacturing control is built.**

## What a jewelry business gains by measuring its shop floor

When a workshop goes from running from memory to running with real-time data, the benefits show up fast and in very concrete places:

- **Reliable delivery deadlines.** With real times per phase, you can know whether an order will arrive on time *before* it's too late, and act on the phase that's slowing it down.
- **Real gold control.** Losses stop being a month-end surprise and become data monitored phase by phase, with an owner and a comparison.
- **Complete traceability.** Each piece has a recorded history: where it went, when, who worked on it and with what weight. Essential for quality and for responding to any claim or audit.
- **Decisions based on facts.** The bottleneck, performance by section or the real cost of manufacturing stop being opinions and become numbers on which to decide where to invest and what to improve.
- **Less administrative work.** No more paper sheets, transcriptions to Excel and office hours reconstructing what happened in the workshop.

## In summary

Shop-floor data capture isn't a technological luxury or a digitalization whim. It's what allows a jewelry workshop —an artisanal, demanding environment where the material is worth what it's worth— to be managed with the same precision with which a piece is made.

The gesture is minimal: an operator marking an entry and an exit. The result is enormous: a workshop that knows itself, that knows where each order is, that controls every gram of gold and that can promise deadlines and meet them.

## Frequently asked questions about production tracking

### What is production tracking in jewelry?

Production tracking is the record an operator makes at their workstation when starting and finishing a manufacturing phase. It captures in real time the time of each phase, the gold's entry and exit weight, and who performed each step. It replaces the paper sheet, making the data be born in the workshop at the exact moment it happens.

### How does tracking help control gold losses?

By recording the entry and exit weight at each phase, the system calculates the loss produced at each step and ties it to a specific process and operator. Instead of discovering at the end of the process that grams are missing with no way to attribute them, you see in which exact phase each loss occurred and compare it with the tolerable loss of that process.

### How does the operator record the tracking in the workshop?

The operator can track whichever way is most convenient for their workstation: by reading the barcode or QR code on the manufacturing envelope with a reader, using a tablet or touch screen installed at the workstation, or from their own phone. All options feed the same system without interrupting the work.

### What do you gain over a paper sheet or a spreadsheet?

With paper or Excel the data is entered late and incompletely, so it only serves to confirm what already happened. With tracking, the information is immediate and reflected instantly on the Factory Map, which lets you know real times per phase, control the gold gram by gram and make decisions while the order is still in the workshop.
