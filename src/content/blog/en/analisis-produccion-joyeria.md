# Production analysis in jewelry: from shop-floor data to the decisions that protect your margin

Throughout this series we've traveled a road. In the [first article](https://www.raytecno.es/en/blog/marcajes-produccion-joyeria) we saw how to capture the data on the shop floor with production tracking: letting the information be born at the workstation, at the exact moment. In the [second](https://www.raytecno.es/en/blog/mapa-fabrica-joyeria) we saw how to give it visible form with the Factory Map: seeing the entire workshop, live, at a glance.

Now we close the circle. Because having the data and being able to see it is essential, but it's still descriptive: it tells us *what's happening*. The final step —and the one that separates a workshop run by intuition from one run by facts— is to **analyze** that information to answer the questions that truly keep a jewelry business owner up at night: will we arrive on time? where are we losing money? which phase is slowing us down? how much gold are we losing along the way?

That's the territory of **production analysis through manufacturing orders**. And it's, without doubt, where the workshop's information stops being a record and becomes a management tool.

## The problem: measuring "by eye" costs money, even if you can't see it

A workshop can run for years without measuring anything rigorously. Orders are delivered, payment is collected, things move on. But beneath that apparent normality hide costs that don't appear on any invoice because nobody measures them:

- **Deadlines promised by eye.** Without knowing how long each phase *really* takes, delivery dates are calculated from experience and missed without understanding why. Each delay erodes customer trust.
- **Tolerated bottlenecks.** There's a phase that's always slower, everyone knows it, but since it isn't measured, it's assumed to be inevitable. That phase is setting the pace —and the limit— of all production.
- **Unknown real cost.** The cost of manufacturing a piece is estimated with theoretical times. If reality deviates 30%, 50% or 300% from what was planned —and it does— the margin you thought you had isn't the one you have.
- **Assumed gold losses.** In jewelry, every gram counts. If loss is only controlled globally, abnormal losses in specific phases dilute into the total and are accepted as "normal."

The common denominator is that **what isn't measured can't be improved or defended**. And in a sector where the material is gold and margins are tight, managing by eye isn't a matter of tidiness: it's a silent profitability leak.

## Analyzing a plan: a complete X-ray of a manufacturing order

The analysis starts from the most concrete: a plan, a manufacturing order. With the tracking already captured, the system reconstructs that plan's **real life** and presents it in a way that's understood in minutes.

**Real cycle times: process versus wait.** For each phase of the route, the analysis distinguishes two very different times: the time the piece is being *worked* (process) and the time it's simply *waiting* between one phase and the next. This distinction is revealing, because in most workshops the waiting time multiplies the working time. Seeing it in numbers —and in a bar per phase— is usually any manager's first "I can't believe it."

**Flow efficiency.** From that relationship between process and wait comes a direct indicator: what percentage of the total time the product is actually advancing versus how much it's stopped. It's an honest measure of how agile the workshop is.

**The bottleneck, proven with numbers.** The analysis points to the phase that concentrates the most wait: the plan's real bottleneck. It's no longer a suspicion shared in the workshop, it's data. And knowing for certain which phase slows everything down is the first step to deciding where it's worth investing.

**Critical line and joint delivery.** A plan usually has several lines in parallel. The analysis identifies the **critical line** —the one that finishes latest— because it's the one that determines when the complete plan can be delivered. Optimizing any other line doesn't bring the delivery forward; only the critical one matters.

**Theoretical/real deviation.** The system compares the hours each phase *should* have cost with what it really cost. When the deviation is +300% or +1,100%, it's not an error: it's the difference between the cost you had budgeted and the real cost you're absorbing. That figure, previously invisible, is pure gold for management.

## Gold control: weight balance per plan

No jewelry production analysis would be complete without following the metal. The analysis offers a **gold weight balance** for the plan: how much **entered**, how much **exited** and how much **loss** occurred, with the resulting **yield** as a percentage.

This turns loss control from a month-end calculation into a management metric. It allows comparing real yield with expected yield, detecting plans or processes that lose more metal than reasonable and, ultimately, putting figures to something that in jewelry is direct money. Every tenth of a gram recovered, multiplied by the gold price and by production volume, is margin that stays home.

## The delivery traffic light: will we arrive on time?

From the whole analysis, an answer to the most important question for the customer is distilled: **will this plan arrive on time?** The **delivery traffic light** answers it with a color:

- **Green**: on time.
- **Amber**: at risk (for example, on date but with material pending receipt).
- **Red**: late, with the specific days of deviation.

And it doesn't stop at the color: when consulted, it explains **why** —the critical line, the bottleneck, the days of deviation, the missing materials. This allows you to anticipate: act on a plan in amber *before* it turns red, instead of discovering the delay when there's no margin left.

Added to this is the cross-reference with **suppliers and procurement**: the analysis links the missing material with its suppliers and lead times, so the cause of a supply-related delay is in plain sight alongside the rest of the diagnosis.

All of it, moreover, is **exportable to PDF and Excel**, to take it to a meeting, share it with a customer or integrate it into a management report.

## The climax: the Plant Dashboard, the management view

Analyzing a plan in depth is very powerful for production. But whoever runs a jewelry business rarely wants to open plans one by one: they want to know **how the whole plant is doing**. That's the definitive leap, and the one that turns everything above into a true management tool: the **Plant Dashboard**.

The Dashboard aggregates *all* the plans in process and summarizes them on a single screen designed for decision-making:

- **Global production health.** How many plans are on time, how many at risk and how many late, at a glance. The traffic light, but at the scale of the whole plant.
- **OTD (on-time delivery).** The percentage of plans that meet their committed date: one of the indicators any management recognizes as a thermometer of its production's reliability.
- **The plant's bottleneck.** Not a single plan's, but the phase that appears as the bottleneck again and again across the whole. This data answers the million-dollar question: *if I could only invest in improving one thing, where?* The Dashboard points to it with numbers.
- **Top of at-risk plans.** A prioritized list —the most urgent first— of the plans on which you must act now. Management knows exactly what to dedicate its attention to this morning.
- **Date filter.** All of it bounded to the period of interest —by default, the plans launched in the last three months—, to analyze the relevant snapshot and not the whole history.

It's the difference between looking at trees and seeing the forest. The Plant Dashboard brings the workshop's data into the language of management: are we reliable with deliveries? where is our capacity limit? what demands our attention today?

## What a management team gains by measuring

When a jewelry workshop goes from managing by intuition to managing by data, the benefits go straight to the bottom line:

- **Reliable, met deadlines.** Knowing whether a plan will arrive on time *before* it's too late allows you to act and deliver, which translates into customers who come back.
- **Protected margins.** Knowing the real cost versus the estimated one, and the real gold loss, prevents you from continuing to sell with a margin you thought you had and didn't.
- **Well-directed investment.** The plant's bottleneck tells you exactly where an improvement will have real impact, instead of investing where it "seems."
- **Fast, well-judged decisions.** The Dashboard puts on the table each morning which plans are at risk and why, to act with focus.
- **A company that doesn't depend on anyone's memory.** Knowledge of production's status is in the system, measured and comparable, not in one person's head.
