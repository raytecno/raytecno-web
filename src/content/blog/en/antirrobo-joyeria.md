When people talk about loss prevention, the first thing that comes to mind is the gate at the door that beeps on the way out. It makes sense in a clothing store, where customers take garments off the shelf. **In a jewelry store, it doesn't**: pieces sit in locked display cases and come out one at a time, in a salesperson's hand, to be shown. And the paper tag with the chip comes off in a second.

A jewelry store's losses happen elsewhere. To see them, you have to look where they happen.

---

## 1. The four losses that don't go through the door

| Loss | Where it happens | How you see it |
|---|---|---|
| **The piece that goes out and doesn't come back** | At the counter, when it is shown | Knowing at all times which piece is out, with whom and since when |
| **The switch for an imitation** | At the counter or on a return | Weighing the piece and comparing it with its factory weight |
| **The shift-change shortfall** | In the case, between shifts | Counting the case on every shift, with two signatures |
| **Internal theft** | At the till | In discounts, refunds and voids |

The last one is the least visible and the heaviest: in a University of Florida survey, jewelers attributed **41% of their shrinkage to employees**.

## 2. What the industry recommends

Industry associations and insurers have been repeating the same practices for years:

| Practice | Who recommends it |
|---|---|
| **One piece out at a time**: show one, lock the case before taking out another and check it before putting it back | Jewelers' Security Alliance and American Gem Society |
| **Daily two-person count**: one person counts and another verifies; always open and close with two people | Jewelers Mutual |
| **Watch till exceptions**: what isn't charged isn't in sales, but it is in discounts, refunds and voids | Retail loss-prevention tools |

The problem has never been knowing what to do, but doing it every day without it depending on anyone's goodwill.

## 3. Three controls: display case, counter and head office

**The display case, on every shift.** With an RFID reader, counting a case means passing the reader over it: a few seconds. At opening, shift change and closing, one person counts and another verifies. Whatever is missing after a check belongs to the next shift, and a badly done check is stored with who signed it.

**The counter, piece by piece.** Every piece taken out of the case is assigned to the salesperson, with the time. One at a time; if it stays out too long, an alert goes off. And because the chip is in the tag and not the jewel, it is weighed when it comes back: **an imitation doesn't weigh the same**, and a difference of a few hundredths of a gram is enough to spot it. That requires knowing the weight of each unit, something a manufacturer has and a buyer from suppliers doesn't.

**Head office, in the numbers.** From the office, store by store and salesperson by salesperson: high discounts, cash refunds or refunds without a receipt, voided sales, receipt reprints, till shortfalls, missing pieces, pieces that didn't come back. Compared with the chain average. **One cash refund is not theft; nine, when the average is three, is something to look at.**

## 4. What it doesn't solve

Better to say it clearly:

- Smash-and-grab or armed robbery is a matter for the store's protocol and the cameras.
- If nobody weighs the piece, a switch with the tag included gets through.
- A tag torn off and left in the case still reads as present.

No tool replaces the routine. What it can do is make sure the routine is followed and that, when it is skipped, someone knows.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a security gate work in a jewelry store?

Not much. A gate works when the customer takes the product off the shelf. In a jewelry store pieces sit in locked cases and come out one at a time in the salesperson's hand, and a paper tag with a chip comes off in a second. Losses happen at the counter, at shift change and at the till.

### How do you detect a switch for an imitation?

By weighing the piece. The chip is in the tag, not the jewel, so the reader won't notice the switch. The scale will: if the piece has its factory weight, it is compared when it goes back into the case, with a tolerance of hundredths of a gram.

### How do you detect internal theft?

In the till figures, not in the display case. What isn't charged doesn't appear in sales, but it does in discounts, refunds and voids. Comparing each salesperson with the chain average flags what is worth a look, without accusing anyone.
