Ask a jeweler what a ring is worth and you get a number. Ask how they got to that number and out comes a list: the gold in the piece at the day's fixing, with its loss allowance and fineness; the stones and components; the hours of each section at its hourly rate; what is added for selling, distribution and commissions; and the profit. **The price of a piece of jewelry is not a number: it is a structure.**

The problem is that most systems store only the result. And the result cannot answer, months later, the questions that really matter.

---

## 1. What is inside a price

An example of how the price of a gold piece breaks down, out of every 100 of structure:

| Concept | Out of 100 | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Gold** | 46 | Model weight plus loss allowance, by fineness and the day's fixing |
| **Components and stones** | 14 | The piece's bill of materials, at cost |
| **Labour** | 18 | The routing operations, at each section's hourly rate |
| **Surcharges** | 10 | Marketing, sales, distribution and commissions, per the price list |
| **Profit** | 12 | The price-list percentage on the full cost |

The figures change from one piece to another and from one month to the next. That is exactly the point: if they are stored, the changes show.

## 2. Why it must be stored at invoicing

The fixing changes every day and the price list changes with the customer. If the structure is not stored at the moment of sale, recalculating it later gives something else: another gold price, other times, other surcharges.

That is why the useful thing is to store it **on every line sold, at invoicing**, with the gold price on the invoice date and the price list it was sold under. From there, the same information can be viewed piece by piece, on the invoice itself, or added up by month and by karat.

## 3. Three questions the structure answers on its own

**Did I charge what the structure gave?** The difference between what was invoiced and what the structure gave tells you which sales were made above or below the price list. The ones that stray furthest are the first to look at.

**Do surcharges cover the cost they were set for?** Each surcharge exists to pay for something not in the hourly rate. If the sales surcharge has to cover 72,000 a year and by now 54,000 of the expected 66,000 has been collected, it is covering 82%: the price-list percentage is too low.

**How much gold did I really sell?** Not the gold on the item record, but the model weight with its loss allowance, added up by fineness. It is the figure purchasing needs to restock and management needs to understand the margin.

## 4. The gold you have at stake

A jewelry business doesn't just sell gold: it holds it in stock, in the factory, at workshops and in what customers owe it. All that gold, at today's price, is money that rises and falls with the market.

With 5 kg of fine gold at 100 per gram, the company has 500,000 at stake. **If gold drops 1%, it loses 5,000** without selling a single piece. Knowing it every day is the difference between deciding when to lock in a price and finding out at month-end.

## 5. Why sales change

Sales up 8% says nothing if you don't know why. With the structure stored, the change can be broken down:

| Why it changes | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sales in the same period last year | 1,000,000 |
| **Gold**: the gold price has gone up | +60,000 |
| **Price**: the same items, more expensive | +15,000 |
| **Pieces**: fewer units sold | −10,000 |
| **Mix**: different items are selling | +15,000 |
| **Sales this period** | **1,080,000** |

Read this way, the 8% is not a sales success: gold contributed three quarters of it, and fewer pieces are being sold.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the price structure of a piece of jewelry?

It is what the price is made of: the gold in the piece at the day's fixing with its loss allowance and fineness, components and stones, labour at each section's hourly rate, price-list surcharges for selling, distribution and commissions, and the profit.

### Why isn't storing the price enough?

Because the price is the result. Months later, without the structure, you cannot tell how much of what you sold was gold, how much was labour, or whether a sale was made above or below what the price list gave.

### What is the gold you have at stake?

The gold the company owns today: in stock, in the factory and at workshops, and owed to it, minus what it owes. Multiplied by the gold price, it tells you how much the company gains or loses if gold moves 1%.
